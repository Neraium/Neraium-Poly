# Paper execution

The exchange-neutral `OrderBroker` protocol supports immutable `OrderIntent` submissions and cancellation. `LiveBroker` always raises, including when environment flags change. The paper broker also refuses startup unless paper mode is enabled and live mode is disabled.

`PaperBroker(risk, path=':memory:', config=ExecutionConfig())` owns a durable order ledger and snapshot liquidity budget. The risk ledger is mandatory. `submit(intent, decision, book, now=..., fee_rate=None, maker=False)` returns an `ExecutionReport`. Default 100 ms latency means a later `process(client_order_id, book, now=...)` is required to match. Integration scenarios may explicitly configure zero latency. Duplicate submissions return the stored report without creating a new execution event. Changed identifiers, economics or execution terms reject.

Matching consumes at most 10% of displayed ask depth by default, in ascending price order up to the limit. Snapshot identity includes exchange, token, sequence and event timestamp; conflicting content under the same identity rejects. Shared persistent depth prevents two orders or duplicate callbacks from using the same liquidity. Partial orders can match later snapshots with new liquidity. Stale/incomplete books, identity mismatches, future data, missing liquidity, wide spreads and unavailable risk reject. Market-close event processing must cancel pending orders upstream; the broker cannot independently infer a market's lifecycle from a book.

`TradingOpportunity.fee_rate`, broker fee rates, and `submit(fee_rate=...)` are effective proportional **notional bounds**, not the exchange curve coefficient. The default simulated taker rate is 2%, configurable; it is a research assumption and is not a claim about every Polymarket market's fee schedule. The broker charges `fill_notional * fee_rate`; the risk ledger rejects fills exceeding reserved fees. Maker orders must initially rest, must see a later market event, and consume a separate conservative participation cap (default 2%). Maker fees default to zero. This deterministic queue approximation omits queue position, cancellations ahead, adverse selection and maker rebates; it is not execution certification.

A proposed cumulative fill and depth consumption are written durably before touching risk capital. The broker then posts a cumulative idempotent risk acknowledgement and finalizes its report. Recovery consults risk audit records before retrying: a crash after capital posting cannot double-fill. Unknown acknowledgement errors retain pending state and consumed liquidity, preventing further matching until recovery. Definitive risk rejection withholds that snapshot liquidity and rejects the order. Cancellation and expiry wait for pending acknowledgement resolution. Persistent broker and risk databases must be retained together; do not replace either ledger independently.

`cancel(id, now=...)` and `expire(now=...)` release remaining reservations without releasing invested capital. Approval expiry is stricter than order expiry; a new order requires new approval. `history()` gives execution reports. `reconcile(now=...)` resolves pending acknowledgements and compares cumulative broker economics with the risk audit, returning `consistent`, `mismatches`, positions and portfolio metrics. All fills, positions and settlement P/L are simulated. This implementation explicitly rejects SELL; there are no live order, wallet or signing transports.

Verified tests cover latency, depth reuse, partial fills, cancellation, expiry, kill switch, stale and missing data, risk unavailability, excess fees, changed duplicate orders, cross-instance concurrency, lost acknowledgements and process-crash recovery. Unbounded historical liquidity is never assumed.

Recovery hardening: a persistent per-exchange/token watermark rejects decreasing event timestamps and decreasing positive sequence numbers, including after restart. Sequence 0 means unknown; an adapter that resets known sequences needs an explicit reviewed epoch/migration policy rather than silently replaying prior depth. Broker reconciliation checks both directions: every acknowledged risk fill must have a matching broker order and cumulative economics. Any pending acknowledgement, unavailable audit/portfolio, or orphan risk fill returns `consistent=false`, with separate `pending_orders`, `unavailable`, and `mismatches` diagnostics. One broker database must pair with the complete risk ledger; partitioned broker ledgers over a common risk ledger are not independently certifiable.
